# Port scan detection: coverage

Generated by `daemon/portscan/testdata/coverage`. Do not edit by hand:
the table below is produced by replaying the capture corpus through the

## Status codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `D_FULL` | Reliably visible to this sensor. |
| `D_PARTIAL` | Visible, with a documented blind spot. |
| `D_INDIRECT` | The scan itself is not visible; only a side effect is. |
| `U_UNREACHABLE` | **Structurally invisible** from the target host. The reason is stated. |
| `U_OUT_OF_SCOPE` | Detectable in principle, deliberately not covered. The reason is stated. |

`Scan` is whether the activity can be seen. `Attribution` is whether the
address in the packets can be believed to be its origin's. The two differ
for every indirection technique, and that difference is why blocking is
gated by policy rather than automatic.

## Why, in each case

**TCP SYN scan** (`-sS`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
a bare SYN to a port where nothing listens is the nominal case.

**TCP connect scan** (`-sT`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
connect(2) puts the same bare SYN on the wire; the difference is in the scanner, not in the packets.

**TCP FIN scan** (`-sF`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
a FIN outside an established connection cannot be legitimate.

**TCP NULL scan** (`-sN`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
no control bits at all is not something a stack emits.

**TCP Xmas scan** (`-sX`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
FIN, PSH and URG together cannot open or continue a connection.

**TCP Maimon scan** (`-sM`): scan `D_PARTIAL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
FIN|ACK carries ACK, the shape of a connection's teardown, so it is treated as an ACK scan is: counted at the lowest weight, not counted in the ephemeral range, and not enough on its own to block.

**TCP ACK scan** (`-sA`): scan `D_PARTIAL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
a lone ACK is also what a straggler from a just-closed socket looks like, so it is counted at the lowest weight; the connection table removes most of the ambiguity, which a network sensor cannot do at all.

**TCP window scan** (`-sW`): scan `U_UNREACHABLE`, attribution `D_FULL`.
byte-identical to -sA on the wire: the two differ only in how the scanner reads the window field of the reply. Reporting anything more specific would be a fabrication, so it is reported as an ACK scan.

**Custom flag combinations** (`--scanflags`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
anything that is not a legitimate combination is caught as 'not matched above' rather than by a list of known ones, so a novel combination is still caught.

**SYN|FIN scan** (`--scanflags SYNFIN`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
a classic filter-evasion combination, named explicitly.

**UDP scan** (`-sU`): scan `D_PARTIAL`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
legitimate multi-port UDP traffic is common (mDNS, SSDP, games, P2P) so the weight is low; and a UDP source address costs nothing to forge, so RequireTCPEvidence refuses to block on UDP-only evidence.

**IP protocol scan** (`-sO`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
counting distinct IP protocol numbers is cheap and unambiguous; the method is legitimately mixed because -sO also emits real TCP and UDP headers.

**Decoy scan** (`-D`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
the scan is fully visible but the forged sources are indistinguishable from the real one. This is the reason the default block policy asks rather than acts.

**OS fingerprinting** (`-O`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
mixes a port scan with fingerprinting probes, so several probe families arrive from one source.

**Fragmented probes** (`-f / --mtu`): scan `D_PARTIAL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
the first fragment ends after the eighth byte of the TCP header, so the ports are readable and the flags are not. The scan is detected; the technique behind it is reported as indeterminate rather than guessed, because an unread flag octet reads as zero and would otherwise be reported as a NULL scan.

**Slow scan** (`-T0 / -T1`): scan `D_PARTIAL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
a leaky bucket detects a scan whose probes arrive faster than it leaks, but how fast is not the whole answer: as the interval approaches the point where gain equals leak, the time to reach the threshold diverges. Config.SlowestDetectableScan reports the slowest scan the running configuration reports WITHIN AN HOUR, which is the figure worth quoting; slower scans are either invisible or detected long after the scanner has gone.

**Idle (zombie) scan** (`-sI`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
every probe reaches us from the zombie, so the scan is plainly visible; the attacker never sends us a packet, so their address is not in anything we can observe.

**FTP bounce scan** (`-b`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
the connections arrive from the FTP server. Largely obsolete, but the attribution limit is the same as the idle scan's.

**Spoofed source** (`-S`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
the source address is chosen by the sender; nothing in the packets contradicts it.

**SCTP INIT / COOKIE ECHO scan** (`-sY / -sZ`): scan `U_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, attribution `D_FULL`.
IP protocol 132 is counted towards the protocol-scan total, but SCTP chunk parsing is not implemented: it would add a third transport parser to the kernel program for a technique that is vanishingly rare on desktops.

**Horizontal scan of the network**: scan `U_UNREACHABLE`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
a sweep of one port across many hosts is, from any single host, one packet. There is nothing to count. This is a property of the vantage point, not a gap in the implementation.

**Single-port brute force**: scan `U_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, attribution `D_FULL`.
repeated attempts on one port are a credential attack or an application failing to reach a stopped service, not reconnaissance. Counting them is the mistake PortSentry and flowhawk make.

**IPv6 port scan** (`-6`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
identical to the IPv4 techniques once the target address is known; address-space size protects the network, not the host.

**IPv6 FIN scan** (`-6 -sF`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
the flag analysis is address-family independent.

**IPv6 Xmas scan** (`-6 -sX`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
the flag analysis is address-family independent.

**IPv6 UDP scan** (`-6 -sU`): scan `D_PARTIAL`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
same weighting and same refusal to block on UDP-only evidence as IPv4.

**Extension-header evasion** (`Topera`): scan `D_FULL`, attribution `D_FULL`.
hiding the TCP header behind more IPv6 extension headers than the parser walks (eight, and 1024 bytes) is itself the signature: the host answers such a probe, ordinary traffic never sends one, so each packet that exhausts the walk scores like a SYN to a system port and the scan is reported as ipv6-extension-evasion. The ports themselves are not readable, and the probes are not evidence for blocking.

**Out-of-band enumeration** (`DNS / rDNS / certificate logs`): scan `U_UNREACHABLE`, attribution `U_UNREACHABLE`.
no packet reaches the host at all. Detecting it is not a network-sensor problem.

## What the running configuration implies

These are derived from the configuration, not asserted: the slowest scan a
leaky bucket can detect is a function of its threshold, its decay rate and
the weight of one probe. As a scan slows towards the rate at which the
bucket leaks, the time it takes to be reported grows without bound, so each
figure below is the slowest scan reported within an hour of its first probe.

Score threshold 100, decay one point per 10s.

| Probe | Weight | Slowest detectable interval |
|---|---|---|
| SYN to a closed system port (< 1024) | 12 | 1m36s |
| SYN to a closed registered port | 6 | 47s |
| SYN to a closed dynamic port | 3 | 23s |
| anomalous flags, system port | 16 | 2m9s |
| UDP to a closed dynamic port | 1 | 7s |

For reference, `nmap -T1` waits 15s between probes and `nmap -T0` waits
5 minutes. A scan slower than the figures above is reported late or not at
all. A lower threshold or a slower decay moves the figures, at the price of
reporting ordinary traffic; no setting makes a leaky bucket see a scan that
is slower than its leak.
